# payTrace on real and public fraud data

Generated by `python -m src.report_real` (2026-11-07 12:08). Reproduce: download the two Kaggle datasets into `data/raw/`, then run `python -m src.adapters` (see `src/adapters.py`), `python -m src.experiments_real`, `python -m src.experiments_real --finish` and `python -m src.train_ieee_native`.

## Why

The first payTrace model was trained on synthetic data I generated. Applied to outside data it fails (first row of each table): it flags almost every payment. Models only learn the fraud that is in their training data.

## Data

| Dataset | What it is | Payments | Customers | Fraud rate | Period | Test period (unseen) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sparkov** (Kaggle `kartik2112/fraud-detection`, CC0) | Simulated US card payments (third-party Sparkov generator) | 1,852,394 | 999 | 0.52% | 2019-01-01 → 2020-12-31 | 523,388 payments, 2,008 fraud |
| **IEEE-CIS** (Kaggle competition, Vesta) | **Real** card-not-present payments, anonymised | 590,540 | 217,779 (reconstructed) | 3.50% | 6 months | 128,823 payments, 4,393 fraud |

Amounts are converted to rupees at a fixed rate (₹88 per US$) so everything shares the app's currency; the model's behavioural features are ratios, so the rate barely matters. Personal fields in Sparkov (names, street, job, date of birth) are dropped. IEEE-CIS has no customer id; the proxy used by top competition solutions (card + address + account-open day) is used, and it has no merchant id at all.

## Protocol

Each dataset keeps its own time split: train 0–50%, early stopping 50–60%, calibration 60–67%, thresholds 67–75%, **test 75–100% (never used for training or tuning)**. Combined models are tested on each dataset's own test period. The full payTrace system is used (LightGBM + calibration + Isolation Forests + autoencoder + fusion + review/block thresholds). PR-AUC: 1.0 is perfect, random guessing scores the fraud rate.

| Features | PR-AUC | ROC-AUC |
|---|---|---|

Saved replay model (`models/ieee_native`, thresholds chosen on the 67–75% slice): PR-AUC 0.461, ROC-AUC 0.862; review tier catches 70.0% of fraud at 15.3% precision and 13.69% false alarms; block tier 8.2% recall at 98.1% precision.

## What this shows

1. **The synthetic-trained model does not work on outside data** (PR-AUC 0.11 on Sparkov, 0.067 on IEEE-CIS; it flags nearly everything).
2. **Trained on Sparkov, the same system is strong on Sparkov's unseen months**: PR-AUC 0.941, 98.8% of fraud caught with 1.70% false alarms. This is the model the web app now uses. Sparkov is simulated, so this is strong evidence, not proof on real payments.
3. **On real IEEE-CIS data, the fields a payment form can supply are not enough** (PR-AUC 0.12): most reconstructed customers have one payment and there are no merchant ids. IEEE-CIS's own anonymised columns carry far more signal; the app replays real IEEE-CIS payments through that model in **Admin → Real data**.
4. **Fraud patterns did not transfer between datasets** (trained on one, tested on the other: near random), and **combining them hurt**: Sparkov PR-AUC fell from 0.94 to 0.66 because IEEE-CIS's larger fraud count dominated training.
5. **Adding payTrace's behaviour features to IEEE-CIS's own columns did not help** (0.394 vs 0.461); training stopped after a few rounds, which suggests those features drift over the 6 months.

## Limits

* No public dataset contains Indian UPI payments with real fraud labels and customer histories. Sparkov is US card data (simulated); IEEE-CIS is real but anonymised and has no merchants.
* Sparkov has no device data and each customer's city never changes, so the app's model cannot learn device or location signals from it; the bank policy rules still use them.
* Every model learns the fraud in its own data. The Sparkov model is very good at Sparkov-style fraud (mid-sized card payments, often at night) and gives only moderate scores to amounts far larger than any fraud it saw, which is why the policy rules (limits, unverified payees, extreme amounts) stay in front of it.
* The three-way combination (Sparkov + IEEE-CIS + synthetic) ran out of memory in the 8 GB build workspace and was not retried: combining two datasets already made results worse.
