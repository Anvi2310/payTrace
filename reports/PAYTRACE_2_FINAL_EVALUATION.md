# payTrace 2.1: final evaluation

Generated by `python -m src.v21.report` from `reports/v21/*.json`. Splits are chronological on each dataset's own timeline: train 0–50%, early stopping 50–60%, calibration 60–67%, thresholds 67–75%, **test 75–100% (never used for fitting, early stopping, calibration or thresholds)**. Loss = missed fraud value + ₹100 per false alarm. Three kinds of data are kept apart: **Sparkov** (simulated card payments, the app's model), **IEEE-CIS** (real Vesta card-not-present payments, anonymised) and **synthetic UPI-style** data (our generator, used where devices, P2P transfers and labelled fraud patterns are needed). Nothing here was validated on live banking infrastructure.

**Why did '+behaviour' score 0.394 in 2.0?** It stopped at iteration 9 (early-stopping patience 100). With patience 200 the same 470 features train to 1949 iterations and score 0.473, above raw columns alone. Behaviour features whose distribution shifts between the training and early-stopping slices (PSI > 0.25): m_age_hours, m_conf_fraud_rate, m_new_payer_share_24h, d_new_accounts_24h, m_users_24h, m_round_share_24h. Constant on IEEE-CIS (no P2P, one channel): channel_code, is_p2p, u_new_city, u_in_amt_1h, u_in_cnt_1h, u_passthrough. Removing the unstable ones did not help (0.469).

**Feature selection (train slice only):** 431 raw columns; median missing share 61.7%, 53.4% of columns over 50% missing; dropped 1 >99% missing, 1 constant, 18 V columns >0.98 correlated within the same missing pattern; kept 411 raw + engineered = 442 features.

Top features by gain (raw + engineered): uid_conf_fraud_rate_past, uid_conf_fraud_past, C1, id_20, C14, DeviceInfo, id_19, D2, V189, TransactionAmt, card2, P_emaildomain.

**About the delayed fraud label.** `uid_conf_fraud_past` counts earlier payments of the same card holder whose fraud label is at least 7 days old (a chargeback arriving). It is past-only and realistic for a bank that learns of chargebacks, but on IEEE-CIS it is strong because the dataset labels every later payment of a defrauded account as fraud, while a real bank would usually have blocked the card. The label-free result is the conservative improvement; the 30-day variant shows sensitivity to how fast labels arrive.

**Which model runs in the app's Real-data replay.** `raw_plus_engineered` (LightGBM, default settings) was fixed in advance as the main experiment; it is NOT the best test score in the table (unweighted and tuned LightGBM score a little higher), because picking the winner by its test score would leak the test period into model selection. Learner choice matters little (LightGBM, XGBoost and CatBoost are within 0.005 PR-AUC on the same features); the engineered features matter a lot. `selected_spw20` stopped at iteration 2 (heavy class weighting makes the early-stopping loss rise at once), so its score reflects early stopping, not the weighting itself.

## 7. Limitations

* Sparkov and the synthetic UPI-style data are simulated; IEEE-CIS is real but anonymised. Real-bank performance will differ.
* The app's model (Sparkov) has never seen devices or P2P transfers; in the app, device and mule signals come from the ring monitor and policy rules.
* Unseen-fraud and ring results are on our own generator; the ring rules were designed with knowledge of its patterns.
* The IEEE-CIS gain depends mostly on delayed fraud labels of the same card holder; the label-free gain is smaller (section 2).
* No live validation, no production deployment claims.
